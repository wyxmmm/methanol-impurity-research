# Research Question

## Current question

> To what extent can literature-derived impurity concentration, catalyst family, and operating conditions predict methanol performance loss during CO2 hydrogenation and methanol synthesis?

## Plain-language meaning

This project asks how much contaminants in the gases used to make methanol reduce catalyst performance. It also asks whether results from published experiments can be organized well enough to predict that effect under different conditions.

## Variables being considered

Possible predictors include:

- impurity identity and concentration;
- catalyst composition;
- temperature and pressure;
- H2:CO2 ratio or syngas composition;
- space velocity or contact time;
- time on stream;
- how the impurity was introduced.

Possible outcomes include methanol yield, selectivity, productivity, formation rate, and catalyst deactivation. These measurements are not automatically interchangeable.

## Scope

The project focuses on experimental methanol-synthesis studies. Papers about catalyst mechanisms and realistic gas-stream impurities can support the analysis, but they are kept separate when they do not measure methanol performance directly.

The question remains provisional because the final wording will depend on how many compatible measurements remain after manual extraction and duplicate checking.
